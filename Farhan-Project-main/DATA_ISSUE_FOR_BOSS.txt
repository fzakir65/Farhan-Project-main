SUBJECT: Data status check before we build the perfume AI

Hi,

Before we go further on the perfume AI project, I need to flag something
about the dataset we were given. I want to be straight with you so we
don't waste time training a model on the wrong foundation.

================================================================
THE SHORT VERSION
================================================================

The spreadsheets we have are well-organised, but most of the content
inside them looks AI-generated rather than collected from real customers
or real perfumers. The structure is fine. The substance is thin.

If we train a model on this as-is, the model will look great in our own
tests and then fail the moment a real customer types a real sentence.

================================================================
WHAT WE ACTUALLY HAVE
================================================================

Four Excel files containing:

  1. A list of 315 fragrance "accords" (e.g. amber, citrus, woody).
     Catalogue: clean and useful.

  2. A list of 883 individual notes (e.g. bergamot, sandalwood, oud).
     About half of them have full chemistry data; the other half are
     name-only stubs.

  3. A table of 1,656 rows that says "for each accord, here is how
     much of each note to use."
     Looks like data. Is actually a formula. The numbers were generated
     by multiplying three columns together (importance x presence x
     compatibility) and then forcing the layers to add up to a fixed
     ratio. There is no perfumer behind these numbers.

  4. A table of 10,000 customer requests, each one paired with the
     "right" accord that the system should return.
     Looks like data. Is actually templated. The customer text reads
     like fill-in-the-blank: "I'm 32 and Looking for something that
     fits night_out." The descriptors and occasions are usually
     written verbatim into the sentence, so any model can guess them
     by keyword matching.

  5. A list of 450 real perfumes (Dior Sauvage, Bleu de Chanel, etc.).
     This part is real and useful, but it lives in a different
     vocabulary from the rule tables (see "Issue 3" below).

================================================================
WHAT WE ARE LACKING
================================================================

Issue 1: No real customer language.
  All 10,000 customer requests read like they came from a script.
  Real customers say things like "I want something that smells like a
  hotel lobby in winter" or "remind me of my grandfather's leather
  jacket." None of that variety is in the data. We have no way to
  learn how real people describe scents.

Issue 2: No real perfumer recipes.
  The dosage table is a single auto-generated version (literally
  labelled "v0.1-auto"). It is not the work of a perfumer. It is a
  formula a script ran. Training a model to copy this formula adds
  nothing - the formula is already in the spreadsheet.

Issue 3: Two parallel worlds that don't meet.
  The "rule" side and the "real perfume" side use different ingredient
  lists. Out of 883 ingredients, only 50 appear on both sides. So when
  the system suggests a recipe, that recipe will use ingredients that
  no real perfume on record actually uses. Same for accords: only 32
  out of 315 are used on both sides.
  This is why the "reference perfume" tag in the training data only
  matches the labelled accord 1.9% of the time. The two halves of the
  spreadsheet weren't built to talk to each other.

Issue 4: No safety / regulatory data.
  The handoff document talks about IFRA limits, allergen lists, and
  reactivity rules. None of that data exists in the spreadsheets. If
  we ever want to ship a real product, we need this. For now we can
  proceed without it as long as we are clearly building a prototype,
  not something that will go on a shelf.

Issue 5: Customer feedback is mostly missing.
  Out of 10,000 example requests, only 1,731 have a rating and 1,508
  have an "accepted/rejected" flag. Real recommendation systems learn
  from feedback. Ours can't yet.

================================================================
IS THERE A WORKAROUND, OR DO WE NEED REAL DATA?
================================================================

Honest answer: a mix. Some of the gaps we can paper over with
engineering. The most important ones we cannot.

What we CAN work around right now:

  - The templated-text problem can be partly fixed by scraping public
    perfume reviews from sites like Fragrantica or Basenotes. Those
    reviews are real customer language. Doing this would give the
    text-understanding part of the model a fighting chance.

  - The two-vocabulary problem can be fixed by us, manually, by
    matching the rule ingredient list to the real-perfume ingredient
    list. It's a few hours of careful work, not a research project.

  - The auto-generated dosage formula can be used as a "starting
    point" rather than a "ground truth." The model nudges the formula
    based on user preferences instead of trying to invent dosages from
    scratch.

  - Safety data can be skipped while we are in prototype mode, as long
    as we are clear with everyone that the prototype is not for
    public/commercial use.

What we CANNOT work around:

  - We cannot make a model good at "real perfumer judgment" without a
    real perfumer. At some point, someone with a trained nose has to
    look at the dosage tables and tell us where the auto-formula gets
    it wrong. A weekend with one good perfumer would dramatically
    improve the data quality.

  - We cannot validate that the system "smells right" without a panel
    of real noses to evaluate the outputs. No amount of math fixes
    this. If the project is heading anywhere serious, we need a small
    panel (even five trained noses) to score outputs.

  - We cannot ship a product without IFRA / regulatory data. That data
    has to be entered from official sources. It's curation work, not
    AI work.

================================================================
WHAT I RECOMMEND
================================================================

Three options, in order of cost:

  Option A - Prototype only, no new data.
  Build the system on what we have. Be very clear it's a research
  prototype. It will demo well on the templated examples and probably
  underperform on real user input. Cost: zero new data. Timeline:
  weeks. Risk: false confidence.

  Option B (recommended) - Prototype plus targeted data top-up.
  Build the system AND in parallel:
    - Scrape ~20,000 real perfume reviews for natural language.
    - Pay one perfumer for two days to review and correct the dosage
      table on the top 50 most-used accords.
    - Bridge the two ingredient vocabularies (us, internally, ~1 day).
  This is the cheapest path to a system that works on real users.
  Cost: small. Timeline: same overall, runs in parallel.

  Option C - Real data foundation.
  Hold off on heavy modelling. First, commission proper data:
    - Real customer briefs paired with real perfumer formulas.
    - IFRA-mapped ingredient list with safety limits.
    - A small evaluation panel.
  Cost: real budget and time. Timeline: months. Outcome: actually
  shippable.

I think Option B is the right call. We get a working demo on the
current timeline, while quietly fixing the two biggest data gaps with
modest investment. Option A risks looking good in our slides and
falling apart in front of a real user. Option C is the right answer
for a final product, but we don't need it yet to prove the idea.

Happy to walk through any of this in person.

Thanks.
