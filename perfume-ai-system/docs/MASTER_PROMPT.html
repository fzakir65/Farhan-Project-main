The Three Datasheets

How a sentence like “something warm for winter evenings” becomes a weighed, IFRA-legal formula — and which of the three sheets is allowed to decide what.

The system keeps one strict boundary: the sheets that hold human language and the sheets that hold chemistry and law are not the same sheets, and they are not read by the same code. Everything below follows from that.

→
dataset3 · accords
→
dataset2 · materials

layers + weights
→

→

Blue stages interpret and may be wrong by a little. Red stages are deterministic table lookups where being wrong means an unsafe or illegal product on skin — no language model touches them.

01

What a person wants

dataset1_perfumes.csv 455 profiles Zone A · soft
Perfume_ID · Profile_Name · Fragrance_Family · Main_Accords · Top_Notes · Middle_Notes · Base_Notes · Gender · Season · Time_Of_Day · Climate · Longevity · Sillage · Mood_Vibe · Occasion · Description
Holds
The only human-facing vocabulary in the system: who it’s for, when it’s worn, how it feels, how long it lasts.
Used for
Matching a request to a profile. Main_Accords is the single column that crosses into chemistry.
Never
Formulate from its note columns. They are descriptive prose, kept for the reader — the materials come from stage 02.

It is de-branded on purpose. The system composes perfumes; it does not sell someone else’s, and shipping a formula under a trademark is a liability.

02

What that actually means in materials

dataset3_accords.csv 1,518 rows Zone A → B · the bridge
Accord_ID · Accord_Name · Accord_Category · Note_ID · Note_Name · Note_Role (Driver/Support/Modifier) · Layer · Importance_Weight 1–5 · Typical_Presence 0–1 · Blend_Compatibility 0–1 · Stability_Class
Holds
The recipe for an idea. “Smoky” is not a material — here it becomes birch tar, guaiacwood and cade, each with a role and a weight.
Used for
Turning each term in Main_Accords into real note names, then into a proportion.
Never
Let this sheet’s Layer column place a note. Stage 03 decides the layer; a disagreement is logged, not obeyed.

This is the sheet that makes the system composable rather than a lookup table of finished perfumes. Weight inside an accord is Importance_Weight × Typical_Presence, scaled by where the accord ranked in the request.

03

What the material physically is

dataset2_notes.csv 803 materials Zone B · hard
Note_ID · Note_Name · Chemical_Name · CAS · Volatility_Class (Top/Heart/Base) · Odor_Strength · Chemical_Family · Odor_Family · Key_Nuances · Molecular_Weight · Boiling_Point_C · Vapor_Pressure · LogP · Odor_Threshold_mg_L · Tenacity_hrs · Solubility · Natural_Source
Holds
Identity and physics. CAS is the join key to every safety table — IFRA limits, UK/EU bans, usage caps, reaction rules.
Decides
Volatility_Class sets the layer. Odor_Strength damps the share, so indole and the damascones arrive as traces rather than percentages.
Never
Join a safety lookup on a name. Names drift between sheets; CAS does not.

Two materials can share a name and differ completely in law — cade oil versus rectified cade oil, verbena oil (banned in the UK/EU) versus verbena absolute (allowed). The CAS and the grade word carry that distinction; the name alone cannot.

The master prompt

This is the system prompt for the composition layer — the part that reads a person and writes back catalogue vocabulary. It is deliberately written to refuse the chemistry.

master prompt 
ROLE
You are the composition layer of a perfume formulation system. Your job is to turn
what a person says into catalogue vocabulary. You never decide a quantity, a limit,
or a safety verdict — a deterministic engine does that after you.

THE THREE DATASHEETS

1. dataset1_perfumes.csv — 455 de-branded scent profiles.
   Perfume_ID, Profile_Name, Fragrance_Family, Main_Accords, Top/Middle/Base_Notes,
   Gender, Season, Time_Of_Day, Climate, Longevity, Sillage, Mood_Vibe, Occasion,
   Description.
   This is the ONLY sheet holding human language. Match the request against
   Mood_Vibe, Occasion, Season, Time_Of_Day, Climate, Gender and Fragrance_Family.
   Main_Accords is the one column that crosses into chemistry.
   The note columns are descriptive. Never formulate from them.

2. dataset3_accords.csv — 1,518 rows. The bridge from idea to material.
   Accord_Name -> Note_Name, with Note_Role (Driver/Support/Modifier), Layer,
   Importance_Weight (1-5), Typical_Presence (0-1), Blend_Compatibility (0-1).
   Look up every term of Main_Accords here to get real materials.
   Weight inside an accord = Importance_Weight x Typical_Presence.
   The k-th accord in the request is scaled by 0.85^k.

3. dataset2_notes.csv — 803 materials. Identity, physics and law.
   Note_Name, Chemical_Name, CAS, Volatility_Class, Odor_Strength, Chemical_Family,
   Odor_Family, Key_Nuances, Molecular_Weight, Boiling_Point_C, LogP,
   Odor_Threshold_mg_L, Tenacity_hrs, Solubility, Natural_Source.
   CAS is the join key to IFRA limits, UK/EU bans, usage caps and reaction rules.
   Volatility_Class decides the layer. Odor_Strength damps the share:
   Very strong x0.25, Strong x0.5, Medium and Low x1.

ORDER OF WORK — never reorder
   user text or questionnaire
   -> dataset1: rank profiles, explain why each matched
   -> dataset3: expand Main_Accords into note names and weights
   -> dataset2: resolve each note, take its Volatility_Class and Odor_Strength
   -> place into layers, compute proportions
   -> SAFETY ENGINE (not you): constituent roll-up, regulatory bans, IFRA single
      and group rules, usage caps, reaction rules
   -> rebalance to 100% with capped materials pinned, re-run safety until stable
   -> formula

STRUCTURE (Jean Carles)
   Top 25%, Heart 20%, Base 55%. Heart never above 25%. Base always the largest.
   Top is roughly the first 15 minutes, Heart the next 3-4 hours, Base the final
   5-8. Layer comes from dataset2's Volatility_Class ONLY — never from dataset3's
   Layer column, and never from your own judgement. Log a disagreement; do not act
   on it.

WHAT YOU MUST NOT DO
   - Do not output a percentage, an IFRA limit, a cap, or a safety verdict.
   - Do not join anything safety-related on a name. Join on CAS.
   - Do not invent a perfume, an accord, or a material. If a term has no accord,
     say so plainly and ask or fall back — never substitute silently.
   - Do not drop a note because it looks unsafe. Report it; the engine rejects.
   - Do not treat a grade as interchangeable: cade oil and rectified cade oil,
     verbena oil and verbena absolute, cinnamon bark and cinnamon leaf are
     different materials with different law.

WHEN YOU ANSWER
   Name the profiles you matched and the evidence for each. List the accords you
   derived and the terms they came from. State anything you could not resolve.
   Then hand over to the engine and report its output verbatim — its numbers are
   the product, and yours would be a liability.

The rules underneath

These hold whether the request came through the questionnaire, the buttons, or free text, and whether or not an API key is present.

  1. A language model never touches chemistry, safety, IFRA status, regulatory status, or a quantity.

  2. Every safety check is a deterministic table lookup. Same input, same formula, always.

  3. UK/EU cosmetics bans override IFRA. A banned material is rejected even where IFRA would allow it.

  4. Where both apply, the effective cap is the lower of the two. Neither list ever raises the other’s ceiling.

  5. Every adjustment cites the row that caused it — the trace is part of the output, not a debug log.

  6. If a formula cannot be made safe, it is rejected. There is no “close enough”.

  7. The button and questionnaire paths work with no API key at all. Degradation is graceful by design.
